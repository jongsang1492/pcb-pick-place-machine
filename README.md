## Project Summary

This project involved the **mechanical design and development of a desktop PCB Pick and Place machine** intended for educational and small-scale automated PCB assembly.

The machine was designed as a compact alternative to industrial-scale pick-and-place systems, with emphasis on:

- precision motion
- modular architecture
- mechanical simplicity
- component versatility

The final concept uses a **gantry-style X-Y motion system** combined with a **rack-and-pinion Z-axis lifting mechanism**, enabling controlled pick-and-place motion in a compact desktop form factor.

## Design Engineering Process

The project followed a structured engineering design workflow.

### 1. Problem Definition
The goal was to design a **desktop-sized automatic pick-and-place machine** for educational PCB assembly, rather than a full industrial production system.

### 2. Engineering Specifications
Key specifications were established through research and system planning, including:

- compact desktop footprint
- support for a range of PCB sizes and component types
- reliable component pickup and placement
- acceptable motion precision for educational use
- reasonable prototype cost

### 3. Concept Generation
Multiple design alternatives were developed for:

- X-Y motion
- Z-axis motion
- nozzle configuration
- reel feeding method

### 4. Concept Selection
Alternative systems were evaluated using engineering criteria such as:

- precision
- cost
- space footprint
- service life
- setup time
- compatibility
- assembly rate

This structured concept selection process helped justify the final architecture rather than choosing a design arbitrarily.

## Alternative Design Development

Several design alternatives were explored before the final architecture was selected.

These included concepts such as:

- multi-nozzle turret-style pick head systems
- rack-and-pinion Z-axis concepts with interchangeable nozzles
- robotic arm based pick-and-place concepts
- different reel feeding and planar motion architectures

By comparing multiple concepts early in the project, the final system was selected on the basis of engineering trade-offs rather than preference alone.

---

## Final Mechanical Architecture

The final machine concept consisted of the following major subsystems:

### X-Y Motion System
- gantry-style motion architecture
- timing belt driven planar motion
- synchronized stepper motor driven movement

### Z-Axis Motion System
- **rack-and-pinion vertical lifting mechanism**
- guide-supported vertical motion for alignment and stability
- stepper motor driven actuation for controlled up/down travel

### Pick Head
- dual-nozzle configuration
- interchangeable nozzle concept
- rotational capability for component orientation adjustment

### Reel Feeder System
- modular reel feeder arrangement
- automatic component feeding concept
- sensor-assisted dispensing concept for tape advancement

## Z-Axis Rack and Pinion Mechanism

One of my main contributions was the design of the **rack-and-pinion Z-axis mechanism**, which drives the vertical motion of the pick head.

<p align="center">
  <img src="images/z_axis_mechanism.png" alt="Rack and Pinion Z-Axis Mechanism" width="600">
</p>

This mechanism was designed to provide:

- reliable vertical motion
- compact mechanical packaging
- straightforward integration with the pick head assembly
- controlled motion transfer from motor rotation to vertical travel

Key design considerations included:

- rack and pinion alignment
- structural support of the moving assembly
- motion smoothness
- mechanical simplicity and maintainability

This was a strong mechanical design problem because the Z-axis had to integrate motion transmission, packaging constraints, and placement accuracy into a compact subsystem.
